0–5 min · Start with the patient

Infection?Compatible syndrome and a meaningful specimen.
Urgency?Shock, organ dysfunction, immune status.
Likely activity?Prior isolates, recent antibiotics, local data.
Source?Drain, remove, relieve obstruction; confirm drug exposure.
Reassessment?Review cultures, narrow, and set a stop plan.

A resistant culture does not establish infection. A rectal CRE screen or a chronically positive tracheal aspirate may indicate colonization. Asymptomatic bacteriuria usually needs no antibiotic; pregnancy and endourologic procedures involving mucosal trauma are key exceptions.

In severe infection, obtain appropriate cultures without delaying urgent therapy. Use the patient's prior organism and susceptibility results to choose empiric coverage. “History of MDRO” is less useful than “prior NDM-producing Klebsiella.” Request missing susceptibility tests early.

First question for the room: What clinical evidence would make you treat this isolate?

Sources amr, asb

5–10 min · Read the organism, mechanism, and MIC together

Susceptible does not mean interchangeable

Use the drug-specific breakpoint and infection site. A smaller MIC for another drug does not make it a better choice. SDD requires the exposure linked to that category; EUCAST “I” means susceptible with increased exposure.

A gene result is a clue

A detected carbapenemase can redirect therapy before full AST. A negative panel excludes only its targets. Multiple mechanisms may coexist; confirm the phenotype and test the intended drug.

Two distinctions do most of the work today. ESBL and inducible AmpC differ in whether cefepime is a dependable choice. KPC, NDM, and OXA-48-like enzymes differ in which beta-lactamase inhibitor works.

Do not read a matrix color as the patient's susceptibility result. AST means antimicrobial susceptibility testing. Check the isolate, site, dose, and current laboratory interpretation before prescribing.

Sources amr, breakpoints, eucast

Teach these three patterns · 8 minutes

ESBL versus AmpC — For invasive ESBL, start with a carbapenem. For susceptible inducible AmpC, optimized cefepime is a key option. Moderate-risk species include E. cloacae complex, K. aerogenes, C. freundii, and H. alvei.

Match the CRE inhibitor — KPC has several active inhibitor combinations; NDM requires aztreonam-avibactam or cefiderocol; OXA-48-like favors ceftazidime-avibactam. NDM can coexist with enzymes that destroy aztreonam alone.

DTR Pseudomonas — Request AST for ceftolozane-tazobactam, ceftazidime-avibactam, imipenem-relebactam, and cefiderocol. For MBL producers, cefiderocol is the preferred option if active.

Scope exceptions: piperacillin-tazobactam can be an alternative for ESBL complicated UTI in the 2026 guidance; this does not establish a role in ESBL bacteremia. Avoid ertapenem initially in critical illness or hypoalbuminemia. Selected AmpC ceftriaxone continuation exceptions and non-carbapenemase CRE are covered in the optional section.

Teach the site traps · 4 minutes

MRSA — Vancomycin or daptomycin are established bacteremia options. For pneumonia, vancomycin or linezolid; daptomycin is inactivated by pulmonary surfactant.

VRE bacteremia — Linezolid or optimized high-dose daptomycin based on the MIC and patient factors. E. faecium daptomycin SDD is dose dependent, not permission to use a routine low dose. Ampicillin susceptibility is a separate question from vancomycin resistance.

CRAB and Stenotrophomonas — First establish infection. Remember the preferred regimens above; leave salvage combinations for the optional section.

22–28 min · The source changes the treatment

Bladder versus kidney or blood

Nitrofurantoin and oral fosfomycin are not bloodstream or renal-parenchymal drugs. Susceptible ESBL cystitis may be treated with an appropriate oral agent; an ESBL label alone does not mandate IV therapy. Oral transition for invasive infection needs an active, well-absorbed drug with adequate systemic exposure.

Lung versus blood

Daptomycin can treat bacteremia but not alveolar pneumonia. A respiratory CRAB or Stenotrophomonas isolate needs a compatible syndrome before treatment. The matrix cannot distinguish a colonizer from the cause of pneumonia.

Exposure and toxicity

Match dose and infusion to the MIC, site, and renal function. Cefepime accumulation can cause neurotoxicity. Monitor potassium, renal function, and CBC with TMP-SMX; CBC with linezolid; CK with daptomycin. Use pharmacy support for vancomycin and complex dosing.

Control the source, then narrow

Drain collections, relieve obstruction, and address infected devices. Persistent infection warrants a source and exposure review plus repeat susceptibility testing when appropriate. Stop redundant coverage once a reliable active regimen is established; use combinations when there is a specific rationale.

Resistance alone does not determine duration. Base the course on the syndrome, effective treatment, clinical response, and source control. Oral step-down also requires stability, absorption, and a feasible follow-up plan.

Sources amr, daptomycin, linezolid

28–37 min · Three decisions, three minutes each

Case 1 · An ESBL bloodstream isolate

A patient with E. coli bacteremia from obstructed pyelonephritis is hypotensive. The isolate is ESBL-producing and reported susceptible to piperacillin-tazobactam.

What determines the immediate drug and the source intervention?

Reveal teaching point

Use meropenem or imipenem-cilastatin if active and urgently relieve the obstruction. A susceptible piperacillin-tazobactam result does not make it the preferred treatment for ESBL bacteremia. After stability and source control, consider an active oral systemic agent when appropriate, not nitrofurantoin.

Case 2 · Ceftriaxone-susceptible Enterobacter

E. cloacae complex grows in blood. Ceftriaxone and cefepime are reported susceptible.

Why is ceftriaxone susceptibility not the end of the decision?

Reveal teaching point

Inducible AmpC can emerge during treatment. For invasive infection, optimized cefepime is an appropriate option if active and no other mechanism undermines it. Use a carbapenem when indicated by severity, susceptibility, or coexisting ESBL. Check source control and the delivered dose.

Case 3 · A carbapenemase result changes the plan

K. pneumoniae bacteremia is initially treated with ceftazidime-avibactam. The rapid assay detects NDM.

What should change before the complete susceptibility panel returns?

Reveal teaching point

Ceftazidime-avibactam alone does not cover NDM. Promptly choose a mechanism-appropriate regimen guided by prior AST and local access, then confirm current AST. Preferred options are aztreonam-avibactam or cefiderocol. Ceftazidime-avibactam plus aztreonam is an alternative if the fixed combination is unavailable. Do not transfer this algorithm to MBL Pseudomonas.

Sources amr

Begin with five clinical decisions before choosing a drug

Is this infection?Name the syndrome and assess specimen quality.
How urgent is it?Assess shock, organ dysfunction, and immune status.
What is likely active?Review prior isolates, recent therapy, and the local antibiogram.
Will it reach the site?Check tissue exposure, dose, route, and source control.
What changes tomorrow?Set a culture review, narrowing plan, and monitoring plan.

A resistant organism in urine, a chronic wound, or a tracheal aspirate may be colonization. A new compatible syndrome, objective findings, and the quality of the specimen determine whether treatment is warranted. A rectal CRE screen guides precautions and empiric risk assessment; it is not an indication for antibiotics.

Obtain appropriate cultures before antibiotics when feasible without delaying urgent treatment. In severe infection, use prior organism and susceptibility results, recent antibiotic exposure, the likely source, and current local epidemiology to select initial coverage. Prior NDM or KPC infection can change the first drug; a generic history of “MDRO” is insufficient. Ask microbiology to retain invasive isolates and test additional agents early.

Reassess when identification and susceptibility results return. Stop unnecessary MRSA or double gram-negative coverage. A drained collection, relieved urinary obstruction, removed infected catheter, or revised infected device may matter more than adding another antibiotic.

Do not treat an asymptomatic culture

Asymptomatic bacteriuria generally does not need antibiotics, including when the isolate is ESBL-producing or VRE. Established indications include pregnancy and before endoscopic urologic procedures involving mucosal trauma. Pyuria alone does not distinguish infection from colonization. Severe systemic illness requires a clinical evaluation, not automatic dismissal as asymptomatic bacteriuria.

Sources amr, asb, cdc

Mechanisms explain which apparently similar drugs are different

Resistance mechanisms and clinical consequences
MechanismExamplesWhat changes at the bedside
Drug destructionESBLs, AmpC, KPC, NDM, OXA enzymesThe inhibitor must match the enzyme. Ceftazidime-avibactam activity against KPC does not imply activity against NDM.
Altered targetmecA/mecC and altered penicillin-binding proteins; van genes; gyrase mutationsMRSA resists usual antistaphylococcal beta-lactams. Vancomycin resistance does not independently establish ampicillin or daptomycin resistance.

Intrinsic resistance and biofilm are different problems

Enterococci are intrinsically resistant to cephalosporin monotherapy. Ertapenem does not cover Pseudomonas or Acinetobacter. Stenotrophomonas is intrinsically resistant to carbapenems. Biofilm-associated persistence may occur despite a susceptible MIC because routine testing evaluates planktonic organisms; investigate the infected surface or device.

ESBL and AmpC describe beta-lactamases. CRE describes a resistance phenotype or carbapenemase production. KPC and NDM identify mechanisms. MDR describes resistance across several categories. DTR Pseudomonas means nonsusceptibility to all eight traditional agents listed in the Pseudomonas section. These terms are related but not interchangeable.

Sources amr, merino, allium, breakpoints

Species-level identification helps predict resistance emerging during treatment

Enterobacter cloacae complex, Klebsiella aerogenes, and Citrobacter freundii have clinically important inducible AmpC risk. The 2026 guidance adds Hafnia alvei, supported mainly by laboratory data and limited clinical experience. A ceftriaxone-susceptible initial isolate can become resistant during therapy.

For invasive infection, cefepime is a reasonable definitive option when susceptible or SDD and dosed to the required exposure. A carbapenem is appropriate when cefepime is inactive, ESBL co-production is established, or the clinical situation makes cefepime unreliable. Piperacillin-tazobactam is not suggested for invasive infection from these moderate-risk organisms.

Do not apply an old “SPICE” mnemonic uniformly. Serratia marcescens, Morganella morganii, and Providencia species have a lower likelihood of clinically significant AmpC overexpression; generally follow susceptibility testing. In high-burden infection or inadequate source control, cefepime may still be preferred. C. koseri is not C. freundii, and K. pneumoniae is not K. aerogenes.

Nuance without losing the default

Avoid ceftriaxone as the planned treatment of invasive moderate-risk AmpC infection. The 2026 guidance allows completion with empiric ceftriaxone in selected non-severe infections after clear improvement and adequate source control. This exception should not justify ceftriaxone in shock, an undrained collection, or an endovascular infection.

Aztreonam survives MBL hydrolysis, but co-produced serine beta-lactamases can destroy it. Avibactam protects aztreonam from those accompanying enzymes. Avibactam does not inhibit NDM itself. This explains why the combination can work when aztreonam alone and ceftazidime-avibactam alone do not.

Do not routinely add an aminoglycoside or polymyxin once an active beta-lactam regimen is established. Tigecycline and eravacycline are alternatives only for suitable nonbloodstream, nonurinary sites. They are poor choices for CRE bacteremia or UTI.

Guidance use and the US label are not identical

The retrieved US aztreonam-avibactam label is for adults with limited or no alternatives who have complicated intra-abdominal infection, with concurrent metronidazole. IDSA-supported use for NDM bacteremia and other sites extends beyond that label. Do not describe all CRE uses as FDA-approved.

Carbapenem resistance in Pseudomonas does not automatically require a reserve antibiotic

If cefepime, ceftazidime, piperacillin-tazobactam, or aztreonam remains susceptible, an optimized traditional beta-lactam may be appropriate even when carbapenems are resistant. In critical illness or poor source control, a susceptible newer agent is also reasonable. OprD loss can disproportionately affect carbapenems; this is one reason to interpret the complete pattern.

DTR Pseudomonas is nonsusceptible to piperacillin-tazobactam, ceftazidime, cefepime, aztreonam, meropenem, imipenem, ciprofloxacin, and levofloxacin. Request testing of ceftolozane-tazobactam, ceftazidime-avibactam, imipenem-relebactam, and cefiderocol.

For invasive CRAB, use a sulbactam-durlobactam regimen and verify that the isolate is causing disease

CRAB means carbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii complex. A positive respiratory or wound culture is often difficult to interpret. Establish a compatible infection before exposing a patient to a complex regimen.

Preferred treatment is sulbactam-durlobactam plus imipenem-cilastatin or meropenem. Sulbactam has direct antibacterial activity against Acinetobacter; durlobactam protects it from relevant beta-lactamases. The carbapenem is an intentional partner in this regimen, not evidence that carbapenem monotherapy is effective against CRAB.

The 2026 guidance places high-dose ampicillin-sulbactam plus another active agent in a temporary bridging role when sulbactam-durlobactam is not immediately available. The target is 9 g/day of the sulbactam component, equal to 27 g/day of the fixed ampicillin-sulbactam product before renal adjustment. Arrange access to preferred therapy rather than assuming these regimens are equivalent.

If sulbactam-durlobactam resistance or an MBL is identified, obtain specialist guidance for a nonsulbactam combination using available active agents such as cefiderocol, minocycline, or polymyxin B. The optimal salvage regimen is uncertain. Do not generalize OXA-48 Enterobacterales treatment to Acinetobacter OXA carbapenemases.

What ATTACK established

Sulbactam-durlobactam plus imipenem-cilastatin met a prespecified noninferiority endpoint against colistin plus imipenem-cilastatin, with substantially less nephrotoxicity. The mortality confidence interval included no difference. The study does not establish a mortality superiority claim, and most patients had pneumonia.

Sources amr, attack, doses

Stenotrophomonas treatment requires an explicit assessment of the evidence

First determine whether a respiratory isolate represents infection. Carbapenem exposure can select Stenotrophomonas, and meropenem does not treat it. Invasive disease includes clinically established pneumonia, bloodstream infection, and other deep infection; a positive airway culture alone is insufficient.

A substantive 2026 change

The 2026 IDSA guidance favors cefiderocol monotherapy for invasive Stenotrophomonas maltophilia infection. This preference is driven largely by susceptibility, pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic data, and animal models. Comparative human studies have not established cefiderocol superiority.

Alternative approaches include aztreonam-avibactam preferably with a second agent, or combination regimens incorporating active TMP-SMX, levofloxacin, or minocycline. Ceftazidime-avibactam plus aztreonam is an alternative when aztreonam-avibactam is unavailable. Selection depends on the site, susceptibility, toxicities, and access. Do not present TMP-SMX monotherapy as the current default for invasive disease.

After clear and sustained improvement, transition to a single active oral alternative may be considered with specialist review. Minocycline has limited sustained serum exposure and is a poor initial monotherapy choice for bacteremia. Levofloxacin can select resistance during treatment. Monitor potassium, renal function, and CBC with TMP-SMX.

Use current testing criteria. The cited minocycline susceptible breakpoint is at most 1 microgram/mL. Aztreonam-avibactam has no established CLSI or FDA breakpoint for Stenotrophomonas in the retrieved guidance. Do not assign “susceptible” by borrowing the Enterobacterales cutoff.

For serious MRSA infection, national vancomycin guidance favors AUC-guided dosing, generally AUC24/MIC 400-600 when MIC is assumed to be 1 mg/L. BJC uses pharmacy-directed trough monitoring. Follow the current BJC pharmacy/Dorsata pathway, indication-specific targets, and creatinine monitoring; do not equate a single trough with an AUC.

For S. aureus bacteremia, the September 2026 IDSA/ESCMID consensus emphasizes risk stratification and diagnostic evaluation. Adults without deep or metastatic foci after appropriate evaluation generally receive 14 days; persistent bacteremia or an incomplete evaluation can justify longer therapy. Endocarditis, osteomyelitis, and retained infected devices require their own plans. Do not prescribe 4-6 weeks solely because the isolate is MRSA.

Persistent bacteremia is a diagnostic problem as well as a treatment problem

Reassess lines, valves, devices, undrained collections, dosing, and repeat susceptibilities. Daptomycin plus a beta-lactam may be considered as specialist salvage for selected persistent MRSA or VRE infections. Routine upfront combination therapy is not established for every patient; the partner beta-lactam may be intended for synergy rather than independent susceptibility.

Sources mrsa, vancomycin, sab2026, vre-dose, asb, zevtera

Dose and infusion strategy are part of whether the regimen is active

Examples below are for adults with normal organ function and the stated infection context. They are not renal-replacement, CNS, pediatric, or pregnancy dosing tables. Recalculate exposure with changing renal function, obesity, augmented clearance, and extracorporeal support. Pharmacy should confirm the exact product components and administration plan.

Clinical failure should trigger a structured reassessment

When the patient is not improving
QuestionWhat to look for
Was the original diagnosis correct?Colonization, drug fever, inflammatory disease, malignancy, or a second untreated infection.
Is the source controlled?Obstruction, abscess, empyema, infected catheter, endocarditis, prosthesis, or other retained focus.
Was active exposure actually delivered?Missed doses, overly reduced renal dosing, rising clearance, poor absorption, chelation interactions, line problems, or inadequate site penetration.
Has resistance changed?Repeat cultures and AST from clinically relevant specimens; compare with baseline and prior exposure.
Is treatment causing harm?AKI, cefepime neurotoxicity, TMP-SMX hyperkalemia, linezolid cytopenia, daptomycin toxicity, or C. difficile infection.

Resistance alone does not require a longer course. Use the infection syndrome, source control, host, and response to choose duration. For invasive infection initially treated with an inactive drug, reassess the treatment clock from effective therapy. S. aureus bacteremia has clearance-based duration rules. A bladder infection that has already clinically resolved may not need a new course solely because the final report shows resistance to empiric therapy.

An oral transition requires an active oral drug with appropriate systemic/site exposure, stability, source control, and reliable absorption. Fluoroquinolones and TMP-SMX can allow oral completion in selected resistant Enterobacterales infections. That principle does not automatically validate oral treatment for endocarditis, CNS infection, or every resistant bacteremia.

Tell infection prevention promptly about newly identified CRE or other targeted resistance. Follow local contact-precaution and containment procedures, communicate colonization/infection status during transfer, and preserve hand hygiene, equipment cleaning, and device prevention practices. Antibiotics are not routine decolonization treatment for CRE.

Sources amr, sab2026, cdc

The major studies support specific decisions within specific populations

MERINO supports carbapenems for invasive ESBL-type infection

Randomized, open-label noninferiority trial in ceftriaxone-resistant E. coli or K. pneumoniae bloodstream infection. In the primary population, 30-day mortality was 23/187 (12.3%) with piperacillin-tazobactam and 7/191 (3.7%) with meropenem. The risk difference was 8.6 percentage points; the one-sided 97.5% CI upper bound was 14.5%, exceeding the 5% noninferiority margin. Susceptibility-testing limitations and standard infusions complicate interpretation. The study does not answer bladder-only infection or prove all optimized piperacillin-tazobactam regimens equivalent to the studied regimen.

Sources merino

ALLIUM supports cefepime-enmetazobactam for cUTI

Randomized trial with 1,034 treated adults. In the primary analysis set, combined clinical cure and microbiological eradication occurred in 273/345 (79.1%) with cefepime-enmetazobactam and 196/333 (58.9%) with piperacillin-tazobactam. The adjusted difference was 21.2 percentage points (95% CI 14.3-27.9), meeting noninferiority and superiority criteria. This composite is not a mortality endpoint. The study did not compare cefepime-enmetazobactam with a carbapenem and does not establish superiority for ESBL bacteremia.

Sources allium

ATTACK supports a safer preferred CRAB regimen

Randomized phase 3 noninferiority study; 125 patients in the primary CRAB analysis, predominantly pneumonia. Both groups received imipenem-cilastatin. Day-28 mortality was 12/63 (19.0%) with sulbactam-durlobactam versus 20/62 (32.3%) with colistin, difference -13.2 percentage points (95% CI -30.0 to 3.5). The noninferiority margin was 20 percentage points. Nephrotoxicity was substantially less frequent with sulbactam-durlobactam. The wide interval and comparator limit claims about mortality superiority and other infection sites.

Sources attack

VRE dosing evidence is observational

A VA cohort included 911 patients receiving daptomycin, but only 60 received at least 10 mg/kg. Standard-dose therapy was associated with poorer survival than high-dose therapy after adjustment (adjusted hazard ratio 2.58, 95% CI 1.27-4.88). Residual confounding, treatment-selection differences, limited quantitative MIC data, and the selected population limit causal conclusions. The study supports avoiding underexposure; it does not prove daptomycin is superior to linezolid or that every patient should receive more than 12 mg/kg.

Sources vre-dose

A guidance preference is not a comparative trial result

The 2026 cefiderocol preference for invasive Stenotrophomonas rests heavily on preclinical evidence, with small and inconsistent human datasets. Similarly, choices among newer CRE agents frequently rely on mechanism, PK/PD, and observational outcomes. When teaching these choices, state the evidence limitation rather than converting “preferred” into “proven superior.”

Work from the syndrome to a defensible treatment plan

Pause before opening each discussion. Name the next action, drug choice, and information still missing.

Case 1 · A resistant urine culture without symptoms

A stable adult with a chronic urinary catheter has ESBL E. coli in urine obtained for odor. There is no new urinary symptom, fever, hemodynamic change, or planned invasive urologic procedure.

Discuss the decision

Do not treat the culture. Review why it was collected and whether the catheter is needed. Resistance does not convert asymptomatic bacteriuria into infection.

Sources asb

Case 2 · ESBL bacteremia with a susceptible piperacillin-tazobactam report

A patient with fever, hypotension, an obstructing ureteral stone, and E. coli bacteremia has CTX-M detected. Piperacillin-tazobactam is reported susceptible.

Discuss the decision

Use an active carbapenem while arranging urgent urinary decompression. Once stable with source control and complete results, assess an active oral option. The susceptibility report does not negate the invasive ESBL treatment evidence.

Sources merino

Case 3 · Enterobacter initially susceptible to ceftriaxone

E. cloacae complex grows from blood and an undrained intra-abdominal collection. Ceftriaxone is susceptible; cefepime MIC is 2 micrograms/mL.

Discuss the decision

Arrange drainage and select cefepime with appropriate exposure, or a carbapenem when indicated by clinical or resistance factors. Include anaerobic coverage if the source requires it. This is not the low-severity, source-controlled exception for continuing ceftriaxone.

Sources amr

Case 4 · NDM changes the CRE choice

A bloodstream K. pneumoniae isolate carries NDM. Ceftazidime-avibactam was started before molecular results.

Discuss the decision

Ceftazidime-avibactam alone is inadequate. Select aztreonam-avibactam or susceptible cefiderocol; ceftazidime-avibactam plus aztreonam is an alternative if the fixed product is unavailable. Obtain confirmatory AST and source control.

Sources amr

Case 5 · Carbapenem-resistant Pseudomonas still has an older option

A stable patient with a drained source has Pseudomonas resistant to meropenem but susceptible to cefepime.

Discuss the decision

Optimized cefepime can be appropriate. Carbapenem resistance is not synonymous with DTR or a carbapenemase. Check the MIC, exposure, source, and response; do not automatically escalate to a reserve agent.

Sources amr

Case 6 · CRAB in a ventilated patient

New fever, purulent secretions, a new infiltrate, and rising oxygen requirements accompany a CRAB respiratory isolate.

Discuss the decision

Evaluate competing causes and specimen quality. If CRAB pneumonia is established, use sulbactam-durlobactam plus a carbapenem partner and request AST. A prior positive respiratory culture without this syndrome would not justify the same treatment.

Sources amr

Case 7 · Stenotrophomonas after broad-spectrum therapy

A neutropenic patient has compatible pneumonia and S. maltophilia in blood and respiratory cultures.

Discuss the decision

Treat invasive disease promptly with the current guidance-informed approach, favoring active cefiderocol. Assess catheter and pulmonary sources. Explain that this recommendation is not supported by a definitive human superiority trial; alternatives require careful combination and toxicity planning.

Sources amr

Case 8 · VRE with daptomycin MIC 4

A patient has VRE E. faecium bacteremia, daptomycin MIC 4 micrograms/mL, and preserved renal function.

Discuss the decision

Confirm the laboratory interpretation and source. Do not use a low S. aureus dose simply because the report is SDD. Discuss high-dose daptomycin or linezolid, CBC/CK monitoring, clearance cultures, and endovascular evaluation when indicated. Persistent infection requires source and repeat-MIC reassessment.

Sources vre-dose

Use these questions to check the reasoning

1. Does the smallest MIC identify the best antibiotic?

Show explanation

No. Compare the drug-specific breakpoint, achievable exposure, site, and outcomes evidence.

2. Does a negative CTX-M result exclude ESBL?

Show explanation

No. Other ESBL families and other resistance mechanisms can produce a similar phenotype.

3. Which Enterobacterales should make you question ceftriaxone despite initial susceptibility?

Show explanation

E. cloacae complex, K. aerogenes, C. freundii, and, in the 2026 guidance, H. alvei. Severity and source control determine whether the limited continuation exception applies.

4. Why can aztreonam-avibactam work against NDM?

Show explanation

Aztreonam resists MBL hydrolysis; avibactam blocks accompanying serine beta-lactamases. Avibactam does not inhibit NDM.

5. Can you use the NDM Enterobacterales algorithm for MBL Pseudomonas?

Show explanation

No. Cefiderocol is preferred when susceptible for MBL Pseudomonas; aztreonam-avibactam has limited activity.

6. Does every CRE infection require two independently active drugs?

Show explanation

No. Routine combination therapy adds toxicity without established benefit once an active beta-lactam regimen is identified. Specific combinations such as sulbactam-durlobactam plus a carbapenem have separate rationale.

7. Is TMP-SMX monotherapy the 2026 default for invasive Stenotrophomonas?

Show explanation

No. Cefiderocol monotherapy is preferred in the current guidance, with explicitly limited human comparative evidence. TMP-SMX is an alternative component of combination therapy.

8. Can nitrofurantoin complete treatment of ESBL bacteremia from a urinary source?

Show explanation

No. Urinary activity does not provide adequate bloodstream exposure.

9. Does MRSA pneumonia respond to daptomycin?

Show explanation

Daptomycin should not be used for alveolar pneumonia because pulmonary surfactant inactivates it.

10. What laboratory monitoring must accompany TMP-SMX?

Show explanation

Potassium, renal function, and CBC, with timing based on baseline risk, dose, and treatment duration. Review interacting medications.

Suggested documentation

[Syndrome and source], with [organism and relevant mechanism/MIC]. Evidence for infection is [clinical and microbiological findings]. Source control is [completed/pending]. Use [drug, dose, infusion, renal adjustment] because [mechanism and site]. Stop [unnecessary coverage]. Obtain [missing testing]. Monitor [specific toxicities]. Antibiotic resistance · One-page rounds sheet

September 17, 2026 · Teaching companion; faculty/local review pending. See the full module for dosing, evidence, exceptions, and sources.

Start with infection

Name the syndrome, assess severity, review prior isolates, obtain appropriate cultures, and control the source. A resistant colonizer does not need antibiotics.

ESBL and AmpC

Invasive ESBL: carbapenem preferred. Invasive moderate-risk AmpC: active, optimized cefepime or a carbapenem when indicated. Do not transfer the cefepime rule from AmpC to ESBL.

CRE mechanism

KPC: meropenem-vaborbactam, ceftazidime-avibactam, or imipenem-relebactam. NDM: aztreonam-avibactam or cefiderocol. OXA-48-like: ceftazidime-avibactam. Confirm AST.

Pseudomonas

Use an optimized traditional beta-lactam when appropriate and susceptible. DTR: test the four newer agents. For MBL producers, cefiderocol is preferred if active.

CRAB and Stenotrophomonas

CRAB: sulbactam-durlobactam plus a carbapenem. Invasive Stenotrophomonas: 2026 guidance favors cefiderocol alone, with limited human comparative evidence.

MRSA and VRE

MRSA bacteremia: established options include vancomycin or daptomycin; evaluate source and clearance. MRSA pneumonia: no daptomycin. VRE bacteremia: linezolid or optimized high-dose daptomycin based on MIC and patient factors.

Exposure and toxicity

MICs are not cross-drug rankings. Match SDD to dose. Adjust for changing renal function. TMP-SMX: K+, creatinine, CBC. Linezolid: CBC. Daptomycin: CK. Cefepime: renal function and mental status.

Reassess and narrow

Repeat AST for recurrence or persistence. Recheck source control and delivered exposure. Resistance alone does not extend duration. Oral transition requires active drug, site exposure, stability, and absorption.
