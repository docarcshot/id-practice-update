Use the clinical context to interpret resistance

Resident core · 30–45 minutes with discussion

A susceptibility report describes an isolate. Deciding whether it explains the illness, and whether an active drug can reach the infected site, takes clinical information.

Adult bacterial infections for residents and ID fellows. Three fictional cases anchor the core. Mechanisms, exposure, trial appraisals, salvage options, and additional cases are optional reading below the lecture.

Use the three cases for discussion. Teach the highlighted matrix patterns; consult the remaining cells as reference. The advanced section is optional and comes after the resident core.

Start with the patient

Asymptomatic bacteriuria and treatment exceptions

Infection?Compatible syndrome and a meaningful specimen.
Urgency?Shock, organ dysfunction, immune status.
Likely activity?Prior isolates, recent antibiotics, local data.
Source?Is there an obstruction, collection, or infected device that antibiotics alone cannot address?
Reassessment?Review cultures, narrow, and set a stop plan.

A resistant culture does not establish infection. A rectal CRE screen or a chronically positive tracheal aspirate may indicate colonization. Asymptomatic bacteriuria usually needs no antibiotic; pregnancy and endourologic procedures involving mucosal trauma are key exceptions.

In severe infection, appropriate cultures are important, but obtaining them must not delay urgent active therapy. The specific prior isolate matters: a history of NDM-producing Klebsiella is more informative than a chart label of “MDRO.” Previous results and local patterns can guide initial coverage while current testing is underway.

First question for the room: What clinical evidence would make you treat this isolate?

Read the organism, mechanism, and MIC together

Species and resistance phenotypes

Susceptible does not mean interchangeable

Use the drug-specific breakpoint and infection site. A smaller MIC for another drug does not make it a better choice. SDD requires the exposure linked to that category; EUCAST “I” means susceptible with increased exposure.

A gene result is a clue

A detected carbapenemase can redirect therapy before full AST. A negative panel excludes only its targets. Multiple mechanisms may coexist; confirm the phenotype and test the intended drug.

Two distinctions do most of the work today. ESBL and inducible AmpC differ in whether cefepime is a dependable choice. KPC, NDM, and OXA-48-like enzymes differ in which beta-lactamase inhibitor works.

The colors below summarize expected activity and clinical limitations. They are not laboratory calls for an individual isolate. Antimicrobial susceptibility testing (AST), infection site, drug exposure, and the evidence for that syndrome remain separate questions.

Recognize these three patterns

ESBL versus AmpC. Carbapenems are preferred for invasive ESBL infection. For species at moderate risk of inducible AmpC, cefepime is preferred when the MIC is susceptible or susceptible-dose-dependent, the exposure matches the MIC, and no ESBL gene has been identified. This group includes E. cloacae complex, K. aerogenes, and C. freundii; the inclusion of H. alvei rests mainly on laboratory evidence.1

Why the enzyme matters. Several inhibitor combinations retain activity against KPC. In invasive NDM-producing Enterobacterales infection, aztreonam-avibactam and cefiderocol are preferred options; ceftazidime-avibactam plus aztreonam is an alternative if the fixed combination is unavailable. Ceftazidime-avibactam fits a different mechanism, OXA-48-like production. The isolate’s AST remains essential.1

Difficult-to-treat Pseudomonas. Testing the four newer agents can reveal an option that the traditional panel misses. Cefiderocol is preferred for a susceptible MBL producer. A KPC-producing isolate is also a poor fit for ceftolozane-tazobactam: tazobactam does not inhibit KPC.1

Where the site changes the answer. Piperacillin-tazobactam can be an alternative for selected ESBL complicated UTI without critical illness or bacteremia. That exception does not establish efficacy for ESBL bloodstream infection. In critical illness or hypoalbuminemia, meropenem or imipenem-cilastatin is preferred over ertapenem for invasive ESBL infection.1

Account for the infection site

MRSA — Vancomycin or daptomycin are established bacteremia options. For pneumonia, vancomycin or linezolid; daptomycin is inactivated by pulmonary surfactant.

VRE bacteremia — Linezolid or optimized high-dose daptomycin based on the MIC and patient factors. E. faecium daptomycin SDD is dose dependent, not permission to use a routine low dose. Ampicillin susceptibility is a separate question from vancomycin resistance.

CRAB and Stenotrophomonas. Either organism can colonize the airway. A compatible pulmonary syndrome, the quality of the specimen, and evidence of invasive infection determine what the isolate means.

The source changes the treatment

Bladder versus kidney or blood

Nitrofurantoin is not a pyelonephritis or bloodstream drug. Oral fosfomycin is not initial treatment for pyelonephritis or sepsis and is not routine bloodstream therapy. A selected transition after active IV treatment for improving E. coli complicated UTI is a separate use discussed in the antibiotic primer. Oral transition for invasive infection requires an active drug with adequate systemic exposure.

Lung versus blood

Daptomycin can treat bacteremia but not alveolar pneumonia. A respiratory CRAB or Stenotrophomonas isolate needs a compatible syndrome before treatment. The matrix cannot distinguish a colonizer from the cause of pneumonia.

Exposure and toxicity

A susceptible dose-dependent (SDD) MIC assumes a particular exposure; changes in clearance, infusion, or infection site can change the interpretation. Confusion or myoclonus on cefepime raises concern for drug accumulation. Examples of safety monitoring include potassium, renal function, and blood counts for TMP-SMX; blood counts for linezolid; and CK for daptomycin. Vancomycin monitoring is indication-specific.

Control the source, then narrow

An abscess, an obstructed urinary tract, or an infected device can sustain infection despite active therapy. Persistent or recurrent illness raises questions about drainage, delivered exposure, and changing susceptibility. Continued double coverage needs a specific rationale once an active regimen is established.

Resistance alone does not determine duration. Base the course on the syndrome, effective treatment, clinical response, and source control. Oral step-down also requires stability, absorption, and a feasible follow-up plan.

Use three cases to practice treatment decisions

Case 1 · An ESBL bloodstream isolate

A patient with E. coli bacteremia from obstructed pyelonephritis is hypotensive. The isolate is ESBL-producing and reported susceptible to piperacillin-tazobactam.

What determines the immediate drug and the source intervention?

Reveal teaching point

The obstruction needs urgent decompression. In this hypotensive patient with ESBL bacteremia, active meropenem or imipenem-cilastatin is preferred even if piperacillin-tazobactam tests susceptible. Later, relief of obstruction, clinical stability, a susceptible systemic oral option, and reliable absorption may permit an oral switch. Nitrofurantoin does not provide adequate bloodstream exposure.115

Case 2 · Ceftriaxone-susceptible Enterobacter

E. cloacae complex grows in blood. Ceftriaxone and cefepime are reported susceptible.

Why is ceftriaxone susceptibility not the end of the decision?

Reveal teaching point

An initially susceptible E. cloacae complex isolate can become resistant during ceftriaxone therapy. For invasive infection, cefepime is preferred when the MIC and achievable exposure support it and no ESBL gene has been identified. A carbapenem may fit better if another mechanism undermines cefepime. The source and its control remain part of that decision.1

Case 3 · A carbapenemase result changes the plan

K. pneumoniae bacteremia is initially treated with ceftazidime-avibactam. The rapid assay detects NDM.

What should change before the complete susceptibility panel returns?

Reveal teaching point

NDM hydrolyzes ceftazidime, and avibactam does not block NDM. The current drug therefore needs prompt reassessment. For suspected invasive NDM-producing Enterobacterales infection, prior testing and available active agents guide urgent treatment while current AST is pending. Aztreonam-avibactam and cefiderocol are preferred options; ceftazidime-avibactam plus aztreonam is an alternative when the fixed product is unavailable. MBL-producing Pseudomonas is a separate problem.1

Begin with five clinical decisions before choosing a drug

Is this infection?Name the syndrome and assess specimen quality.
How urgent is it?Assess shock, organ dysfunction, and immune status.
What is likely active?Review prior isolates, recent therapy, and the local antibiogram.
Will it reach the site?Check tissue exposure, dose, route, and source control.
What changes tomorrow?Set a culture review, narrowing plan, and monitoring plan.

A resistant organism in urine, a chronic wound, or a tracheal aspirate may be colonization. A new compatible syndrome, objective findings, and the quality of the specimen determine whether treatment is warranted. A rectal CRE screen guides precautions and empiric risk assessment; it is not an indication for antibiotics.

Appropriate cultures can clarify the source but must not delay urgently needed treatment in severe infection. Prior susceptibility results, recent therapy, and local epidemiology help estimate activity while tests are pending. A prior NDM-producing isolate and a prior KPC-producing isolate imply different options even though both may be called CRE. Additional testing of a retained invasive isolate can resolve a discordant result.

Identification and susceptibility results can explain why broad empiric coverage is no longer needed. If bacteremia or fever persists, an undrained collection, urinary obstruction, or infected device may explain failure better than a gap in antibacterial spectrum.

Do not treat an asymptomatic culture

Asymptomatic bacteriuria generally does not need antibiotics, including when the isolate is ESBL-producing or VRE. Established indications include pregnancy and before endoscopic urologic procedures involving mucosal trauma. Pyuria alone does not distinguish infection from colonization. Severe systemic illness requires a clinical evaluation, not automatic dismissal as asymptomatic bacteriuria.

Intrinsic resistance and biofilm are different problems

Enterococci are intrinsically resistant to cephalosporin monotherapy. Ertapenem does not cover Pseudomonas or Acinetobacter. Stenotrophomonas is intrinsically resistant to carbapenems. Biofilm-associated persistence may occur despite a susceptible MIC because routine testing evaluates planktonic organisms; investigate the infected surface or device.

ESBL and AmpC describe beta-lactamases. CRE describes a resistance phenotype or carbapenemase production. KPC and NDM identify mechanisms. MDR describes resistance across several categories. DTR Pseudomonas means nonsusceptibility to all eight traditional agents listed in the Pseudomonas section. These terms are related but not interchangeable.

Species-level identification helps predict resistance emerging during treatment

Enterobacter cloacae complex, Klebsiella aerogenes, and Citrobacter freundii have clinically important inducible AmpC risk. The 2026 guidance adds Hafnia alvei, supported mainly by laboratory data and limited clinical experience. A ceftriaxone-susceptible initial isolate can become resistant during therapy.

For invasive infection caused by these moderate-risk species, cefepime is a preferred option when the isolate tests susceptible or SDD, the regimen supports that exposure, and no ESBL gene has been identified. Cefepime resistance or an identified ESBL may favor a carbapenem. Piperacillin-tazobactam is not suggested for invasive disease caused by this group.1

Do not apply an old “SPICE” mnemonic uniformly. Serratia marcescens, Morganella morganii, and Providencia species have a lower likelihood of clinically significant AmpC overexpression; generally follow susceptibility testing. In high-burden infection or inadequate source control, cefepime may still be preferred. C. koseri is not C. freundii, and K. pneumoniae is not K. aerogenes.

When empiric ceftriaxone has already been started

Ceftriaxone is generally unsuitable as planned treatment of invasive infection caused by these moderate-risk species. Finishing a course that began empirically with ceftriaxone is a narrow exception in nonsevere infection after clear clinical improvement and adequate source control. Shock, an undrained collection, and an endovascular focus do not fit that exception.

Aztreonam survives MBL hydrolysis, but co-produced serine beta-lactamases can destroy it. Avibactam protects aztreonam from those accompanying enzymes. Avibactam does not inhibit NDM itself. This explains why the combination can work when aztreonam alone and ceftazidime-avibactam alone do not.

Guidance use and the US label are not identical

The U.S. aztreonam-avibactam indication is adult complicated intra-abdominal infection with limited or no alternatives, in conjunction with metronidazole.10 Its preferred role for NDM-producing Enterobacterales bacteremia is guidance-supported and off-label. It is not established as superior to cefiderocol in a head-to-head trial.1

Carbapenem resistance in Pseudomonas does not automatically require a reserve antibiotic

If cefepime, ceftazidime, piperacillin-tazobactam, or aztreonam remains susceptible, an optimized traditional beta-lactam may be appropriate even when carbapenems are resistant. In critical illness or poor source control, a susceptible newer agent is also reasonable. OprD loss can disproportionately affect carbapenems; this is one reason to interpret the complete pattern.

DTR Pseudomonas is nonsusceptible to all eight traditional options in its definition: piperacillin-tazobactam, ceftazidime, cefepime, aztreonam, meropenem, imipenem, ciprofloxacin, and levofloxacin. Testing ceftolozane-tazobactam, ceftazidime-avibactam, imipenem-relebactam, and cefiderocol addresses a different question: does an active newer agent remain?

When a carbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter isolate explains the illness

CRAB refers here to carbapenem-resistant Acinetobacter baumannii complex. A respiratory or wound isolate can represent colonization. In a seriously ill patient with a compatible invasive syndrome, organism-specific susceptibility testing informs definitive therapy but need not delay initial active treatment.

For invasive CRAB, the preferred combination is sulbactam-durlobactam with imipenem-cilastatin or meropenem. Sulbactam acts directly on Acinetobacter; durlobactam protects it from certain beta-lactamases. The carbapenem is a partner in this combination, not evidence that it works alone. The U.S. sulbactam-durlobactam label covers adults with hospital-acquired or ventilator-associated bacterial pneumonia due to susceptible A. baumannii-calcoaceticus complex. Other infection sites are outside that indication.117

If sulbactam-durlobactam cannot be obtained immediately, high-exposure ampicillin-sulbactam with another active agent has a temporary bridging role. The relevant amount is the sulbactam component, not the combined product weight. This is a contingency while the preferred drug is being obtained, not an equivalent definitive regimen.

If sulbactam-durlobactam resistance or an MBL is identified, obtain specialist guidance for a nonsulbactam combination using available active agents such as cefiderocol, minocycline, or polymyxin B. The optimal salvage regimen is uncertain. Do not generalize OXA-48 Enterobacterales treatment to Acinetobacter OXA carbapenemases.

What ATTACK established

Sulbactam-durlobactam plus imipenem-cilastatin met a prespecified noninferiority endpoint against colistin plus imipenem-cilastatin, with substantially less nephrotoxicity. The mortality confidence interval included no difference. The study does not establish a mortality superiority claim, and most patients had pneumonia.

Stenotrophomonas treatment requires an explicit assessment of the evidence

First determine whether a respiratory isolate represents infection. Carbapenem exposure can select Stenotrophomonas, and meropenem does not treat it. Invasive disease includes clinically established pneumonia, bloodstream infection, and other deep infection; a positive airway culture alone is insufficient.

Why the preference for cefiderocol is qualified

For established invasive S. maltophilia infection, cefiderocol alone is the preferred approach when active. That preference draws heavily on susceptibility, drug-exposure data, and animal models; comparative human studies have not established superior outcomes.1

Alternative approaches include aztreonam-avibactam preferably with a second agent, or combination regimens incorporating active TMP-SMX, levofloxacin, or minocycline. Ceftazidime-avibactam plus aztreonam is an alternative when aztreonam-avibactam is unavailable. Selection depends on the site, susceptibility, toxicities, and access. Do not present TMP-SMX monotherapy as the current default for invasive disease.

After clear and sustained improvement, transition to a single active oral alternative may be considered with specialist review. Minocycline has limited sustained serum exposure and is a poor initial monotherapy choice for bacteremia. Levofloxacin can select resistance during treatment. Monitor potassium, renal function, and CBC with TMP-SMX.

Use current testing criteria. The cited minocycline susceptible breakpoint is at most 1 microgram/mL. Aztreonam-avibactam has no established CLSI or FDA breakpoint for Stenotrophomonas in the retrieved guidance. Do not assign “susceptible” by borrowing the Enterobacterales cutoff.

For E. faecium, a daptomycin MIC of 4 micrograms/mL falls in the CLSI susceptible dose-dependent category (≤4 micrograms/mL). This category assumes increased daptomycin exposure. For adult S. aureus bacteremia without a deep or metastatic focus after adequate evaluation, the usual duration is 14 days from blood-culture clearance. If the focus is removed later, that later date may be the starting point. Persistent bacteremia or an incomplete evaluation may justify a longer course. Endocarditis, osteomyelitis, and retained infected devices require separate assessment. Methicillin resistance alone does not establish the duration.8

Persistent bacteremia is a diagnostic problem as well as a treatment problem

Reassess lines, valves, devices, undrained collections, dosing, and repeat susceptibilities. Daptomycin plus a beta-lactam may be considered as specialist salvage for selected persistent MRSA or VRE infections. Routine upfront combination therapy is not established for every patient; the partner beta-lactam may be intended for synergy rather than independent susceptibility.

What an exposure-dependent result means

Antibiotic toxicity monitoring

Why the MIC alone is incomplete. A susceptible dose-dependent result assumes a defined level of drug exposure. Renal clearance, infusion duration, and reliable delivery can change whether the patient reaches it.

The adult regimens linked below assume normal organ function unless specified. Renal replacement, changing clearance, CNS infection, pregnancy, pediatric care, and home infusion may require a different reference or delivery plan.

Resistance alone does not require a longer course. Use the infection syndrome, source control, host, and response to choose duration. For invasive infection initially treated with an inactive drug, reassess the treatment clock from effective therapy. S. aureus bacteremia has clearance-based duration rules. A bladder infection that has already clinically resolved may not need a new course solely because the final report shows resistance to empiric therapy.

An oral transition requires an active oral drug with appropriate systemic/site exposure, stability, source control, and reliable absorption. Fluoroquinolones and TMP-SMX can allow oral completion in selected resistant Enterobacterales infections. That principle does not automatically validate oral treatment for endocarditis, CNS infection, or every resistant bacteremia.

Tell infection prevention promptly about newly identified CRE or other targeted resistance. Follow local contact-precaution and containment procedures, communicate colonization/infection status during transfer, and preserve hand hygiene, equipment cleaning, and device prevention practices. Antibiotics are not routine decolonization treatment for CRE.

The major studies support specific decisions within specific populations

MERINO supports carbapenems for invasive ESBL-type infection

Randomized, open-label noninferiority trial in ceftriaxone-resistant E. coli or K. pneumoniae bloodstream infection. In the primary population, 30-day mortality was 23/187 (12.3%) with piperacillin-tazobactam and 7/191 (3.7%) with meropenem. The risk difference was 8.6 percentage points; the one-sided 97.5% CI upper bound was 14.5%, exceeding the 5% noninferiority margin. Susceptibility-testing limitations and standard infusions complicate interpretation. The study does not answer bladder-only infection or prove all optimized piperacillin-tazobactam regimens equivalent to the studied regimen.

References 15

ALLIUM supports cefepime-enmetazobactam for cUTI

Randomized trial with 1,034 treated adults. In the primary analysis set, combined clinical cure and microbiological eradication occurred in 273/345 (79.1%) with cefepime-enmetazobactam and 196/333 (58.9%) with piperacillin-tazobactam. The adjusted difference was 21.2 percentage points (95% CI 14.3-27.9), meeting noninferiority and superiority criteria. This composite is not a mortality endpoint. The study did not compare cefepime-enmetazobactam with a carbapenem and does not establish superiority for ESBL bacteremia.

References 16

ATTACK supports a safer preferred CRAB regimen

Randomized phase 3 noninferiority study; 125 patients in the primary CRAB analysis, predominantly pneumonia. Both groups received imipenem-cilastatin. Day-28 mortality was 12/63 (19.0%) with sulbactam-durlobactam versus 20/62 (32.3%) with colistin, difference -13.2 percentage points (95% CI -30.0 to 3.5). The noninferiority margin was 20 percentage points. Nephrotoxicity was substantially less frequent with sulbactam-durlobactam. The wide interval and comparator limit claims about mortality superiority and other infection sites.

References 17

VRE dosing evidence is observational

In a retrospective VA cohort of 911 patients with VRE bacteremia treated with daptomycin, only 60 were in the study’s high-dose group (at least 10 mg/kg). Standard-dose treatment was associated with poorer survival after adjustment (adjusted hazard ratio 2.58, 95% CI 1.27–4.88). The threshold identifies the study group; it does not establish an optimal dose. Treatment selection, residual confounding, and incomplete MIC data limit inference. The cohort did not compare daptomycin with linezolid.18

References 18

A guidance preference is not a comparative trial result

The 2026 cefiderocol preference for invasive Stenotrophomonas rests heavily on preclinical evidence, with small and inconsistent human datasets. Similarly, choices among newer CRE agents frequently rely on mechanism, PK/PD, and observational outcomes. When teaching these choices, state the evidence limitation rather than converting “preferred” into “proven superior.”

Eight cases in which the same laboratory result could lead to different decisions

Pause before opening each discussion. Name the next action, drug choice, and information still missing.

Case 1 · A resistant urine culture without symptoms

A stable, nonpregnant adult with a chronic urinary catheter has ESBL E. coli in urine obtained for odor. There is no new urinary symptom, fever, hemodynamic change, or planned invasive urologic procedure.

Discuss the decision

With no compatible symptoms, pregnancy, or planned procedure involving mucosal trauma, the culture is consistent with asymptomatic bacteriuria. Odor and an ESBL result do not establish a treatment indication. The reason for the catheter remains a separate question.5

Case 2 · ESBL bacteremia with a susceptible piperacillin-tazobactam report

A patient with fever, hypotension, an obstructing ureteral stone, and E. coli bacteremia has CTX-M detected. Piperacillin-tazobactam is reported susceptible.

Discuss the decision

Hypotension and bacteremia make this an invasive infection, and the obstructed collecting system needs urgent decompression. Meropenem or imipenem-cilastatin is preferred if active; a susceptible piperacillin-tazobactam report does not outweigh the bloodstream outcome evidence. A later oral switch depends on recovery, source control, absorption, and an active drug with systemic exposure.151

Case 3 · Enterobacter initially susceptible to ceftriaxone

E. cloacae complex grows from blood and an undrained intra-abdominal collection. Ceftriaxone is susceptible; cefepime MIC is 2 micrograms/mL.

Discuss the decision

The undrained collection needs source control. At the stated MIC, cefepime can be a preferred option for this moderate-risk AmpC species when the needed exposure is achievable and no ESBL gene has been identified. A carbapenem may fit better with an additional resistance mechanism. Mixed intra-abdominal infection may also require anaerobic coverage. Bacteremia with an undrained focus do not meet the exception for completing empiric ceftriaxone.1

Case 4 · NDM changes the CRE choice

A bloodstream K. pneumoniae isolate carries NDM. Ceftazidime-avibactam was started before molecular results.

Discuss the decision

Avibactam does not inhibit NDM, so ceftazidime-avibactam alone is inadequate. For this invasive Enterobacterales infection, aztreonam-avibactam and cefiderocol are preferred options pending confirmation of activity; the fixed product’s use for bacteremia falls outside its U.S. label. If it is unavailable, ceftazidime-avibactam plus aztreonam is an alternative. Source control and repeat assessment remain important.110

Case 5 · Carbapenem-resistant Pseudomonas still has an older option

A stable patient with a drained source has Pseudomonas resistant to meropenem but susceptible to cefepime.

Discuss the decision

Meropenem resistance alone does not establish DTR Pseudomonas or identify a carbapenemase. The retained cefepime susceptibility, clinical stability, and drained source make cefepime a reasonable option if the needed exposure is achievable.

Case 6 · CRAB in a ventilated patient

New fever, purulent secretions, a new infiltrate, and rising oxygen requirements accompany a CRAB respiratory isolate.

Discuss the decision

Unlike an isolated positive airway culture, the new infiltrate, hypoxemia, fever, and purulent secretions support pneumonia. Specimen quality and competing causes still matter. For clinically established invasive CRAB infection, sulbactam-durlobactam with meropenem or imipenem-cilastatin is preferred; urgent treatment need not wait for isolate-specific AST. The final result can change that choice.1

Case 7 · Stenotrophomonas after broad-spectrum therapy

A neutropenic patient has compatible pneumonia and S. maltophilia in blood and respiratory cultures.

Discuss the decision

Concordant blood and respiratory isolates together with the compatible pneumonia support invasive disease; urgent treatment is warranted. The lung and any intravascular catheter are possible sources. Active cefiderocol monotherapy is preferred, largely on preclinical evidence. There is no definitive comparative human trial establishing superiority; alternatives have different combination and toxicity considerations.1

Case 8 · VRE with daptomycin MIC 4

A patient has VRE E. faecium bacteremia, daptomycin MIC 4 micrograms/mL, and preserved renal function.

Discuss the decision

For E. faecium, this MIC is susceptible dose-dependent under CLSI criteria. It is not a routine susceptible result for S. aureus. Linezolid and higher-exposure, off-label daptomycin are distinct options. Blood counts, CK, renal function, culture clearance, and possible endovascular infection help distinguish them and guide follow-up. Use these questions to check the reasoning

1. Does the smallest MIC identify the best antibiotic?

Show explanation

No. Compare the drug-specific breakpoint, achievable exposure, site, and outcomes evidence.

2. Does a negative CTX-M result exclude ESBL?

Show explanation

No. Other ESBL families and other resistance mechanisms can produce a similar phenotype.

3. Which Enterobacterales should make you question ceftriaxone despite initial susceptibility?

Show explanation

E. cloacae complex, K. aerogenes, and C. freundii carry moderate risk of inducible AmpC; H. alvei is included chiefly on laboratory evidence. Completing empiric ceftriaxone is a narrow exception for nonsevere infection after improvement and adequate source control.

4. Why can aztreonam-avibactam work against NDM?

Show explanation

Aztreonam resists MBL hydrolysis; avibactam blocks accompanying serine beta-lactamases. Avibactam does not inhibit NDM.

5. Can you use the NDM Enterobacterales algorithm for MBL Pseudomonas?

Show explanation

No. Cefiderocol is preferred when susceptible for MBL Pseudomonas; aztreonam-avibactam has limited activity.

6. Does every CRE infection require two independently active drugs?

Show explanation

No. An active beta-lactam for CRE generally does not benefit from an additional aminoglycoside or polymyxin, which can add toxicity. For NDM-producing Enterobacterales, ceftazidime-avibactam plus aztreonam is a mechanistic pairing when fixed aztreonam-avibactam is unavailable: avibactam protects aztreonam from accompanying serine beta-lactamases.

7. Is TMP-SMX monotherapy the 2026 default for invasive Stenotrophomonas?

Show explanation

No. Cefiderocol monotherapy is preferred in the current guidance, with explicitly limited human comparative evidence. TMP-SMX is an alternative component of combination therapy.

8. Can nitrofurantoin complete treatment of ESBL bacteremia from a urinary source?

Show explanation

No. Urinary activity does not provide adequate bloodstream exposure.

9. Does MRSA pneumonia respond to daptomycin?

Show explanation

Daptomycin should not be used for alveolar pneumonia because pulmonary surfactant inactivates it.

10. What laboratory monitoring must accompany TMP-SMX?

Show explanation

Potassium, renal function, and CBC, with timing based on baseline risk, dose, and treatment duration. Review interacting medications.

A useful handoff says what makes this an infection, what the isolate and mechanism explain, and whether the selected drug can reach the source. Antibiotic resistance · One-page rounds sheet

October 31, 2026 · Teaching companion. The full module explains exposure assumptions, evidence, and exceptions; the original dosing references provide regimens.

Start with infection

Name the syndrome, assess severity, review prior isolates, obtain appropriate cultures, and control the source. A resistant colonizer does not need antibiotics.

ESBL and AmpC

Carbapenems are preferred for invasive ESBL infection. For moderate-risk inducible AmpC species, cefepime is preferred at a susceptible or SDD MIC when exposure is adequate and no ESBL gene has been identified. These are different mechanisms.1

CRE mechanism

For invasive Enterobacterales infection, KPC and OXA-48-like enzymes can both be inhibited by avibactam. KPC also has meropenem-vaborbactam and imipenem-relebactam options. NDM is different: aztreonam-avibactam or cefiderocol is preferred if active; ceftazidime-avibactam plus aztreonam is an alternative if the fixed product is unavailable.1

Pseudomonas

Carbapenem resistance can coexist with susceptibility to cefepime or another traditional beta-lactam. In DTR Pseudomonas, expanded AST is needed. Ceftolozane-tazobactam is a poor fit for KPC or MBL producers; cefiderocol is preferred for a susceptible MBL producer.1

CRAB and Stenotrophomonas

A positive airway culture alone does not establish invasive infection. For invasive CRAB, sulbactam-durlobactam with imipenem-cilastatin or meropenem is preferred; urgent therapy need not wait for AST. For invasive S. maltophilia, the preference for active cefiderocol alone rests mostly on preclinical evidence.1

MRSA and VRE

Vancomycin and daptomycin are established options for MRSA bacteremia; clearance and the infected focus affect duration. Daptomycin should not be used for alveolar pneumonia. For VRE E. faecium bacteremia, linezolid and higher-exposure, off-label daptomycin have distinct MIC and toxicity considerations.131412

Exposure and toxicity

MICs are not cross-drug rankings. Match SDD to dose. Adjust for changing renal function. TMP-SMX: K+, creatinine, CBC. Linezolid: CBC. Daptomycin: CK. Cefepime: renal function and mental status.

Reassess and narrow

Repeat AST for recurrence or persistence. Recheck source control and delivered exposure. Resistance alone does not extend duration. Oral transition requires active drug, site exposure, stability, and absorption.
