# Lecture review, September 15, 2026

Reviewed all four published modules, including their cases, answer explanations, reference lists, and rounds summaries. This was a clinical and editorial review with targeted verification of treatment recommendations, dose examples, recent guidance, and selected trial results. It was not a systematic literature search or independent faculty approval.

## Changes made

### Antibiotics

The bacteremic Klebsiella UTI case now specifies seven total days of effective treatment for an improving, eligible patient. Its previous quotation of the general five-to-seven-day fluoroquinolone recommendation could be misread as a five-day bacteremia course. [IDSA cUTI guideline](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/complicated-urinary-tract-infections/).

Clarified that cefixime's approximately 50% urinary recovery refers to the absorbed dose, not the administered oral dose. Added the product-label reference. [Cefixime label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6d68dbd9-7d75-4ff1-91db-79ff8ae879ec).

Added the cefepime exposure requirement for susceptible-dose-dependent isolates, the limitation when an ESBL gene is identified, and the selected non-severe-infection exception to the general ceftriaxone avoidance advice for AmpC-risk organisms. Replaced the rounds-sheet instruction that susceptibility “overrides memory” with interpretation based on site, mechanism, and dose. [IDSA 2026 AMR guidance](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/amr-guidance/).

Clarified metronidazole's anaerobic spectrum without using the diaphragm as a coverage boundary. Replaced aspiration with lung abscess in a polymicrobial-coverage example. Added the population size and confidence interval to the fosfomycin trial summary. [Original fosfomycin trial](https://academic.oup.com/cid/advance-article-abstract/doi/10.1093/cid/ciag345/8709548). Removed several editorial or unnecessarily categorical phrases while preserving the user's BJC trough-monitoring instruction.

### Positive blood cultures

Reframed the obstructed-urinary-source case to emphasize both active antibiotics and decompression. Specified the gram-negative treatment-duration anchor, including the consensus exception when improvement begins only after source control. [Gram-negative BSI consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8561258/).

Changed persistent-culture wording from an assumed diagnosis to an instruction to investigate source control, drug delivery, and endovascular or metastatic infection. Clarified that the later-focus-removal duration anchor is an S. aureus recommendation; candidemia also requires symptom resolution and evaluation for deep infection. The 2026 S. aureus follow-up-culture and echocardiography recommendations were retained after verification. [IDSA/ESCMID SAB consensus](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/staphylococcus-aureus-bacteremia/).

### Tuberculosis

Aligned ART timing with the HIV module and NIH guidance. Without meningitis, initiate within two weeks when CD4 is below 50 and within two to eight weeks at higher counts. The original eight-to-twelve-week wording came from the cited CDC page, but differed from the companion module and NIH recommendation. Preserved the actual disagreement between NIH and ATS/CDC/IDSA over TB-meningitis timing. [NIH TB/HIV guidance](https://clinicalinfo.hiv.gov/en/guidelines/hiv-clinical-guidelines-adult-and-adolescent-opportunistic-infections/mycobacterium).

Specified the efavirenz-based ART restriction in CDC's four-month rifapentine-moxifloxacin implementation guidance, replacing vague “compatible ART” wording. [CDC four-month regimen guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/71/wr/mm7108a1.htm). Standard, resistant-TB, preventive-treatment, diagnostic, and investigational-treatment sections remain distinct. Updated to version 1.1.

### HIV and opportunistic infections

Made MAC prophylaxis an explicit recommendation when its criteria are met and active disease has been excluded. Specified the CD4 and exposure criteria for histoplasmosis primary prophylaxis and the suppression interval for considering cessation of primary toxoplasma prophylaxis. Retained separate secondary-suppression criteria. [NIH MAC](https://clinicalinfo.hiv.gov/en/guidelines/hiv-clinical-guidelines-adult-and-adolescent-opportunistic-infections/disseminated), [histoplasmosis](https://clinicalinfo.hiv.gov/en/guidelines/hiv-clinical-guidelines-adult-and-adolescent-opportunistic-infections/histoplasmosis), and [toxoplasmosis](https://clinicalinfo.hiv.gov/en/guidelines/hiv-clinical-guidelines-adult-and-adolescent-opportunistic-infections/toxoplasmosis).

Added LATITUDE's randomized population of 306 to distinguish it from the 453 enrolled in the initial phase. Replaced editorial language in the statin paragraph and imprecise cryptococcal wording. Verified and retained the May 2026 PCP prophylaxis criteria, CMV organ-disease distinction, cryptococcal ART delay, and product-specific prevention versus treatment roles. Updated to version 1.1.

## Teaching relevance and review limits

Separated adjacent superscript citations in the antibiotics and blood-culture modules so two references cannot be read as one larger reference number.

Preserved the resident pathways, advanced evidence sections, diagnostic next steps, and case-based structure. The newer-drug and trial sections remain optional advanced reading rather than bedside prescribing instructions. No new institutional policy or medication-access claim was introduced. Local formulary, Dorsata, infusion practice, isolation procedures, and pharmacy approval still require the already identified local review. Some trial verification used original-publication abstracts or indexed excerpts because full text was inaccessible; this review does not certify every external link or every subgroup claim.
