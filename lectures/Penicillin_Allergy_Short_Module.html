Penicillin allergy

Does this history prevent us from using a beta-lactam?

Assess the reaction, choose an appropriate antibiotic, and identify who needs allergy testing.

Resident core / 01

The reaction history determines the next step

A 52-year-old with a susceptible bacterial infection has an amoxicillin allergy label. At age 8, a rash appeared several days into treatment. There were no mouth sores, blisters, breathing symptoms, or hospitalization. The patient has avoided penicillins since then.

Discuss before reading on. What else would you ask, and does this history require avoidance of every beta-lactam?

Record the drug, timing after the dose and after treatment began, symptoms, treatment required, and subsequent tolerated antibiotics. Ask specifically about mucosal injury, skin peeling, hypotension, and organ injury. A label of "rash" is insufficient. [1]

Resident core / 02

Separate intolerance from immediate and severe delayed reactions

Classify the reaction before selecting a test.
HistoryInterpretation and next step
Nausea, diarrhea, headache, or family history aloneNot evidence of an immune-mediated allergy. Clarify the record and discuss removal of an inaccurate allergy label.
Remote, uncomplicated skin eruptionMay qualify for supervised direct oral challenge after risk assessment.
Rapid hives, angioedema, wheeze, or hypotensionPossible immediate allergy. Recent reactions or prior anaphylaxis generally warrant an allergy evaluation that may include skin testing.
Blistering, mucosal lesions, systemic rash with organ injury, or immune-mediated kidney, liver, or blood-cell injuryAvoid the culprit. Obtain specialist advice; do not use the routine low-risk challenge pathway.

Severe delayed reactions include Stevens-Johnson syndrome/toxic epidermal necrolysis and drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS). Standard immediate penicillin skin testing does not establish safety after these reactions. [1] [2]

Resident core / 03

Challenge, skin testing, and desensitization answer different questions

A graded challenge does not desensitize the patient.
ApproachPurpose
Direct oral challengeTests tolerance in a selected low-risk patient without preceding skin testing. Use the approved protocol, observation, trained staff, and rescue treatment.
Penicillin skin testingEvaluates immediate sensitization. A negative result is generally followed by an observed oral challenge to complete the assessment.
DesensitizationCreates temporary tolerance when the needed drug has no suitable alternative and immediate allergy is established or strongly suspected. Requires specialist supervision and uninterrupted scheduled exposure.

Desensitization does not remove the allergy label. Do not use routine desensitization for Stevens-Johnson syndrome/toxic epidermal necrolysis, DRESS, or serious immune-mediated organ injury. [1] [3]

Resident core / 04

A penicillin allergy does not automatically exclude cefazolin

Cross-reactivity depends largely on side-chain structure. The 2022 practice parameter permits cefazolin, a non-cross-reactive cephalosporin, in patients with penicillin anaphylaxis without prior penicillin testing. This does not apply to a cefazolin allergy or justify routine use after a severe delayed beta-lactam reaction. [4]

For the opening case, first confirm the history. A remote benign rash may qualify for direct amoxicillin challenge under the local pathway. If a cephalosporin is the appropriate treatment, a penicillin label alone need not delay its use. Choosing cefazolin and removing a penicillin label are separate decisions. [2] [4]

Read the important pairings

Amoxicillin shares R1 with cefadroxil and cefprozil; ampicillin shares R1 with cephalexin and cefaclor. These two amino side-chain groups are related, which is why amoxicillin–cephalexin is marked S rather than I. Ceftriaxone, cefotaxime, cefpodoxime, and cefepime share R1; cefuroxime has related methoxyimino features. Ceftazidime and aztreonam share R1. [4] [7]

Cefazolin and carbapenems

Cefazolin has no shared R1 side chain with the other drugs shown. A penicillin allergy alone does not establish cefazolin allergy. For meropenem (†), the 2022 parameter permits a carbapenem in patients with penicillin or cephalosporin allergy, including prior anaphylaxis, without routine preceding testing or additional precautions. That recommendation does not establish tolerance in someone with a carbapenem allergy or a severe delayed reaction. The aztreonam–meropenem cell is outside that specific recommendation. [4]

Do not use this chart to select an unsupervised challenge after anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome/toxic epidermal necrolysis, DRESS, or serious immune-mediated organ injury. A history of cephalosporin anaphylaxis requires a separate evaluation even when the proposed cephalosporin has a different R1 side chain. [4]

This is a teaching matrix, not a complete allergy clearance tool. It does not map R2 side chains, beta-lactamase inhibitor allergy, or other penicillins such as penicillin G/V, oxacillin, and piperacillin. Within-penicillin cross-reactivity can involve shared penicillin determinants. Published charts differ in how they classify partial structural similarities; an unmarked pair is not proof of tolerance. [4] [8]

Resident core / 05

Document what was tested and what the result means

After a successful evaluation, update the allergy list as well as the note. Record the drug, dose, date, observation, and outcome; explain the result to the patient and communicate it to the next clinician. Provide instructions for reporting a delayed reaction. An uneventful immediate observation does not guarantee that no later rash will occur. [2] [4]

Discuss a variation. The same patient recalls blistering and oral ulcers. The next step changes to specialist evaluation and avoidance of the culprit, even if the reaction was decades ago. [1]

Resident core / Close

Apply this on the next consult

Before prescribing, describe the reaction. Before challenging, check exclusions and the local protocol. After evaluation, correct the allergy record and explain the result.

Fellow discussion / Optional

PEN-FAST supports risk assessment but does not override exclusions

In adults, PEN-FAST assigns 2 points for a reaction within 5 years or an unknown interval, 2 for anaphylaxis/angioedema or a severe cutaneous reaction, and 1 for treatment required. A score below 3 identifies a lower-risk group; it does not itself remove the label. Unknown treatment history should be handled according to the validated tool rather than assumed absent. [5]

A remote severe cutaneous reaction can produce a deceptively low total. Exclude severe delayed reactions before using a low-risk challenge pathway. [1] [5]

The PALACE randomized trial supports direct oral challenge for selected adults with PEN-FAST below 3. Its findings should not be extended to excluded high-risk phenotypes or an unmonitored inpatient dose. [6]

For immediate penicillin or cephalosporin allergy, the 2022 parameter also permits carbapenems without routine preceding testing. Severe delayed reactions require a separate assessment. Aztreonam and ceftazidime share a side chain, so that pairing needs specific attention. [4]
