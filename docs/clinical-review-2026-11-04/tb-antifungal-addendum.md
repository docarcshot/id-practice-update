## Exact clinical changes

### TB1. Identify the guideline behind the resident short-regimen summary

Original

> Eligible patients may receive six-month BPaLM or BPaL; eligibility, fluoroquinolone susceptibility, prior drug exposure, and toxicity monitoring determine the choice.

Revised

> The 2025 U.S. guideline supports six-month BPaLM or BPaL for eligible patients; fluoroquinolone susceptibility, prior drug exposure, and toxicity monitoring determine the choice. The 2026 WHO update also uses disease severity; see resistant-TB regimens.

The final phrase links to the existing `#resistant` section. Sources T1 and T2. Disposition passed independent source and saved-text verification. This preserves the U.S. recommendation while directing readers to the newer WHO distinction.

### TB2. Correct the overly broad BDLC branch

Original regimen-role cell

> Bedaquiline + delamanid + linezolid + levofloxacin + clofazimine. WHO six-month option for MDR/RR-TB, including additional fluoroquinolone resistance.

Revised regimen-role cell

> Bedaquiline + delamanid + linezolid + levofloxacin + clofazimine. WHO six-month alternative for eligible MDR/RR-TB when BPaLM cannot be used.

Original qualification cell

> Use susceptibility results to simplify to BDLLfx when FQ susceptible or BDLC when FQ resistant. WHO eligibility is broader, including children and pregnancy, but CNS, osteoarticular and disseminated multiorgan disease require other approaches.

Revised qualification cell

> Use susceptibility and severity to select the regimen. In the 2026 WHO update, BDLC is limited to nonsevere pulmonary pre-XDR-TB. WHO eligibility includes selected children and pregnancy, but CNS, osteoarticular and disseminated multiorgan disease require other approaches.

Source T1 for the new severity and BPaLM-ineligible restrictions. Previously published subgroup/site limitations were retained and made explicitly selective; they were not newly certified from an unread WHO table. Disposition passed independent source and saved-text verification. The clinically consequential correction is that fluoroquinolone resistance alone does not justify BDLC in severe pulmonary pre-XDR disease.

### TB3. Explain the changed WHO recommendation without inventing a dose or severity definition

The following paragraph was absent and has been inserted after the short-regimen table.

> The September 2026 WHO update separates pulmonary pre-XDR-TB (MDR/RR-TB with additional fluoroquinolone resistance) by severity: BPaL for nonsevere disease and BPaLC, adding clofazimine, for severe disease. WHO advises against BDLC in severe pulmonary pre-XDR-TB because of resistance amplification. Apply the full WHO eligibility and severity criteria with the TB program. These WHO recommendations are distinct from the 2025 U.S. recommendations above.

Source T1. Added stable reference `#ref-who26`, reference 37, linking the second-edition publication record and actual issuing update. The existing references were not renumbered. Disposition passed independent source and saved-text verification. Severe extrapulmonary disease, component resistance, prior drug exposure, pregnancy and other regimen eligibility questions still require the applicable complete guidance and specialist assessment.

### AF1. Preserve the selected urinary monotherapy exception

Original mechanism-table statement

> Good CNS entry; rapid resistance with monotherapy limits its role to selected combination regimens.

Revised

> Good CNS entry. Usually combined with another antifungal because resistance can emerge with monotherapy; selected symptomatic fluconazole-resistant C. glabrata urinary infections are an exception.

Added an adjacent link to existing reference 1. The coverage table was also reconciled from “Selected Candida and Cryptococcus combinations.” to “Selected Candida and Cryptococcus treatment, usually in combination.” The separate “Do not use as routine monotherapy” caution remains appropriate. Sources A1 and A2. Disposition passed independent source and saved-text verification. The exception is restricted to the species, susceptibility and symptomatic urinary syndromes supported by IDSA, with renal adjustment and toxicity monitoring still necessary.

### AF2. Add a current serious contraindication

After the existing renal accumulation, marrow suppression, hepatotoxicity and GI intolerance sentence, the drug profile now adds

> Known complete dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) deficiency is a contraindication because of fluorouracil-related toxicity.

Source A2, Contraindications and Warnings. This safety statement was absent. Both reviewers independently read the original label; the lead reviewer authorized this final addition before file hashes were taken. No routine universal DPD testing recommendation or rule for partial deficiency was invented. Disposition passed independent source verification and final saved-text check. The HIV reviewer was notified for cross-module reconciliation.
