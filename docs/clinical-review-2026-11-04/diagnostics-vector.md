## Findings retained after verification

H. pylori regimen review retained optimized BQT for 14 days, susceptibility restriction for clarithromycin/levofloxacin, rifabutin formulation differences, test-of-cure timing, and fixed-pack renal/hepatic exclusions. ACG's official regimen summary and product labeling support these distinctions. The AGA refractory-infection advice supports shared decision making after repeated failures; it does not support routinely leaving an initial confirmed infection untreated. No simplified replacement regimen was invented.

Lyme guidance remains the 2020 AAN/ACR/IDSA guideline, still marked current by IDSA at review. Its relevant diagnostic/treatment sections support paired CSF/serum antibody index, low utility of CSF PCR, oral doxycycline for selected nonparenchymal neurologic disease, IV therapy for parenchymal disease, facial-palsy steroid uncertainty, cardiac admission thresholds, arthritis escalation and high-risk-bite prophylaxis. CDC adult tables were checked against the existing doses/durations. STOP-LD's primary abstract confirms fatigue improvement but not cognitive benefit, with four hospitalized adverse events (three placebo); the existing nuanced description was retained. The Fallon author-repository abstract confirms 37 patients, transient week-12 cognitive improvement and 6/23 versus 1/14 drug/line events. These abstracts do not substitute for full-trial review.

Tick treatment checks included [CDC 2025 tularemia MMWR](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/rr/rr7402a1.htm), adult and pregnancy tables and neuroinvasive/delayed-treatment text; [CDC anaplasmosis](https://www.cdc.gov/anaplasmosis/hcp/clinical-care/index.html) and [ehrlichiosis](https://www.cdc.gov/ehrlichiosis/hcp/clinical-care/index.html); [IDSA babesiosis](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/babesiosis/) and [CDC adult dosing/pregnancy guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/babesiosis/hcp/clinical-care/index.html); and the July 2026 CDC [soft-tick](https://www.cdc.gov/relapsing-fever/hcp/soft-tick-relapsing-fever/index.html) and [hard-tick](https://www.cdc.gov/relapsing-fever/hcp/hard-tick-relapsing-fever/index.html) relapsing-fever pages. Existing organism-specific durations, severe babesiosis assessment and limited evidence for B. miyamotoi CNS therapy were retained. CDC's [Heartland/Bourbon testing guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/heartland-virus/hcp/clinical-diagnosis/heartland-bourbon-testing-guidance.html) confirms that doxycycline nonresponse is not mandatory when exposure and cytopenias fit, and specifies the <14-day versus later specimen pathways.

Inpatient fever was checked against [2023 SCCM/IDSA ICU fever guidance](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/new-fever-in-critically-ill-patients/), [2026 SSC timing recommendations](https://www.sccm.org/survivingsepsiscampaign/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-adult-guidelines), and the source sections listed above. The module already correctly distinguishes possible sepsis without shock from probable/definite sepsis or possible shock and identifies the ICU population limitations. The [IDSA neutropenic cancer guideline summary](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/neutropenic-patients-with-cancer/) supports immediate empiric treatment in febrile neutropenia. [Someko et al., 2023](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ace/5/4/5_23013/_html/-char/en) supports the limited diagnostic value of rash/eosinophilia in drug fever; this reference was added beside the broader allergy practice parameter.

FUO review retained consensus-based baseline evaluation, clinical-clue-directed testing and avoidance of nonspecific empiric therapy in stable immunocompetent patients. The [EANM 2024 full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11224117/) supports the qualified 50–60% diagnostic-helpfulness estimate, observational limitations and steroid effects on PET. Actual CDC pages checked included [Q fever](https://www.cdc.gov/q-fever/hcp/diagnosis-testing/index.html), [brucellosis](https://www.cdc.gov/brucellosis/hcp/clinical-overview/index.html), [histoplasmosis](https://www.cdc.gov/histoplasmosis/hcp/algorithm/index.html) and [malaria](https://www.cdc.gov/malaria/hcp/clinical-guidance/evaluation-and-diagnosis.html), including Brucella serology exceptions and three malaria smear sets 12–24 hours apart.
