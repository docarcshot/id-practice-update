# Clinical, editorial and safety review of the lecture catalog

Review date November 4, 2026. Repository baseline `6dee936`. This is an AI-assisted evidence review and correction release authorized by the site owner. It is not faculty sign-off, institutional approval, or legal assurance.

## What this release establishes

All 19 published lectures were read, including their resident and fellow sections, tables, algorithms, cases, questions and print summaries. Clinical reading, primary-source verification, editorial review and browser testing were treated as different tasks. Each saved clinical correction received a separate source-based verification pass. Reviewers corrected source mappings and qualifiers when the independent pass found a problem.

Five modules passed verification of their bounded published teaching scope. Fourteen modules remain partially source-verified. Their corrected claims are supported, but this release does not certify every unchanged dose, interaction, diagnostic estimate, trial result, map entry or linked source. This is a published correction batch within the requested comprehensive audit, not a claim that the entire audit is finished. The detailed records below identify what remains.

An editing date is not a complete clinical-review date. The catalog now gives both the editing date and clinical status. Existing broad clinical/source-review dates were preserved in partial modules. Only the completed ASB, inpatient-fever, OPAT, penicillin-allergy and hepatitis source reviews received a new clinical-review date. Faculty review remains pending throughout.

The first correction batch was published as `8e6f6b1`. The subsequent source review completed allergy and hepatitis and extended verification in the antibiotic primer, recurrent UTI, H. pylori, Lyme and HIV modules. Each continuation retains a separate source and independent-review record.

## Cross-module consistency and editorial decisions

The antibiotic, resistance, gram-negative and recurrent-UTI modules now distinguish a urinary oral-fosfomycin transition strategy from initial pyelonephritis treatment, bloodstream completion and prolonged off-label prostatitis use. The AmpC completion exception is not presented as preferred planned invasive therapy. OVIVA wording agrees between the antibiotic and bone/joint lectures; OPAT still requires syndrome-specific oral eligibility and source control. Daptomycin action language agrees in the primer and OPAT. Flucytosine's DPD contraindication is reconciled between the antifungal and HIV modules. PEN-FAST derivation and external-validation wording agrees between the allergy module and antibiotic primer. HBV treatment in the hepatitis module preserves complete suppressive HIV therapy; the new bepirovirsen pathway does not authorize stopping HIV-active drugs. Selected symptomatic recurrent-UTI prophylaxis is distinguished from asymptomatic bacteriuria and routine catheter prophylaxis.

The bone/joint lecture already correctly distinguished curative 3/6-month staphylococcal DAIR regimens from noncurative suppression; that distinction was retained. The blood-culture lecture already used the 2026 SAB risk framework and was not reverted to an older algorithm. Likewise, the September 2026 NIH DTG/3TC and July 2026 rezafungin statements were checked against the current issuing sources and retained. A recent source was not assumed wrong simply because it differed from older guidance.

The shared notice now explicitly identifies independent clinician education and disclaims implied Washington University/BJC HealthCare endorsement. It links this record instead of implying that all content has passed verification. Regimens, tables, useful colors, clinical cases and practical source-control and follow-up teaching remain. Existing page URLs and section IDs are preserved. Print-only navigation artifacts and a duplicate catalog notice were corrected, and a long reference URL now wraps on mobile.

No editorial wording can establish legal protection. Institutional legal/risk-management review is appropriate if the owner wants a determination about institutional names or affiliation, public clinical-teaching governance, image/reproduction permissions, or whether any example is intended for adoption as institutional policy. This review did not obtain that approval. Local laboratory service availability, formulary restrictions and patient-specific complex regimens remain matters for the applicable clinical service and faculty.

## Remaining work and source limitations

The fourteen partial modules need the claim groups listed above completed before being labeled clinically verified. Priority should remain with exact renal/hepatic and dialysis adjustments, interactions/contraindications, severe or immunocompromised-host regimens, diagnostic thresholds that change management, susceptibility-method/breakpoint interpretation and original trial numerators/limitations. A guideline's citation to a trial is not a substitute for checking the original report.

Several sources were available as original indexed recommendation passages, official summaries, or author manuscripts while the publisher PDF or supplement remained inaccessible. Those access limits are recorded. They were not silently treated as full-text review. The full September 2026 WHO TB document, its operational severity criteria, and the November 3, 2026 WHO diagnostic update still require review. H. pylori rescue evidence, the complete tick-borne atlas provenance, several remaining drug-label/availability statements and the other specified original-study gaps remain unfinished. The allergy matrix and the complete published hepatitis scope were verified in the continuation; those former gaps are closed. Unresolved external reference targets are documented separately from local navigation checks. Even in the completed hepatitis scope, the final AASLD withdrawal-duration/boundary discrepancies require hepatology judgment before any local protocol is adopted; the lecture states them explicitly.
